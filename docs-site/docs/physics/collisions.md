# Collisions

Impurity ions in Flan undergo Coulomb collisions with the background ions and
electrons through the Nanbu collision model. Collisions are turned on with
`imp_collisions = "nanbu"`, and are applied once per time step, just before the
Boris push (see [Impurity Transport](impurity-transport.md#the-main-loop)).

## The Nanbu model

The Nanbu model was introduced in:

> K. Nanbu, "Theory of cumulative small-angle collisions in plasmas,"
> *Phys. Rev. E* **55**, 4642–4652 (1997).
> [doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.55.4642](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.55.4642)

A charged particle moving through a plasma undergoes a huge number of
small-angle Coulomb collisions. Following each one is out of the question,
and Nanbu's insight was that their cumulative effect over a time step
$\Delta t$ can be replaced by a **single** deflection of the relative velocity by an
angle $\chi$. The distribution of $\chi$ depends only on one dimensionless
parameter,

$$
s = \frac{\ln\Lambda}{4\pi}\left(\frac{q_a q_b}{\varepsilon_0 \mu_{ab}}\right)^2
    \frac{n_b}{g^3}\,\Delta t,
$$

where $q_a$ and $q_b$ are the charges of the impurity and the background
species, $\mu_{ab}$ is their reduced mass, $n_b$ is the background density, $g$ is
the magnitude of their relative velocity and $\ln\Lambda$ is the Coulomb logarithm.
$s$ is roughly the number of collision times that fit in $\Delta t$, so it
measures how collisional the step is:

- $s \ll 1$: the step is weakly collisional, and $\chi$ is small.
- $s \gg 1$: the particle has "forgotten" its initial direction, and $\chi$ is
  isotropic.

The model covers the whole range of $s$ in one distribution, which is its main
strength. A Monte Carlo collision step built on it goes smoothly from
weak, small-angle scattering to full isotropization without changing
algorithms.

## Implementation

The model lives in the `Collisions` namespace (`src/collisions.cpp`, and
`src/cuda/collisions.cu` for the GPU). Every alive, charged particle collides first
with the background ions and then with the background electrons (the
`friction_force` test case skips electron collisions). For each species, the
steps are as follows.

### 1. Sample a background particle

The local $n_e$, $T_e$, $T_i$ and flow velocity $\mathbf{u}$ are interpolated to
the particle's position and time. Each Cartesian component of a background
particle's velocity is then sampled from a drifting Maxwellian:

$$
v_{b,j} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(u_j,\ \sqrt{T_b/m_b}\right),
$$

and the relative velocity is $\mathbf{g} = \mathbf{v}_a - \mathbf{v}_b$. The same
flow velocity, from the Gkeyll ion moments, is used for both ions and electrons.
The background masses come from `gkyl_ion_mass_amu` and `gkyl_elec_mass_amu`.

### 2. Calculate *s*

$s$ is calculated with the formula above, with an impurity of charge state $Z$
($q_a = Ze$) and a singly charged background ($q_b = e$, $n_b = n_e$). The
instantaneous $g$ of the sampled pair is used in $g^3$.

The Coulomb logarithm is $\ln\Lambda = \ln(\lambda_D / b_0)$. $\lambda_D$ is the
electron Debye length, and $b_0$ is the impact parameter for 90° scattering
evaluated at the expected value of the relative velocity, $\langle g^2\rangle =
3T_b/\mu_{ab}$:

$$
b_0 = \frac{q_a q_b}{2\pi\varepsilon_0\,\mu_{ab}\,\langle g^2 \rangle}.
$$

### 3. Calculate *A*

The shape of the $\chi$ distribution is set by $A(s)$, the solution of

$$
\coth A - \frac{1}{A} = e^{-s}.
$$

There is no closed form for $A$, so it is linearly interpolated from a table
(`include/nanbu_s_a.h`, generated by `python/calc_nanbu_A.py`) for
$0.01 \le s \le 5$. Outside this range, the asymptotic limits are used:
$A = 1/s$ for $s < 0.01$ and $A = 3e^{-s}$ for $s > 5$.

### 4. Sample the deflection angle

With a uniform random number $U \in (0, 1]$, the deflection angle is sampled by
inverting the cumulative distribution:

$$
\cos\chi = \frac{1}{A}\ln\!\left(e^{-A} + 2U\sinh A\right).
$$

For $s < 0.02$, the small-$s$ limit $\cos\chi = 1 + s\ln U$ is used, and for
$s > 6$ the deflection is isotropic, $\cos\chi = 2U - 1$. 

### 5. Update the velocity

$\mathbf{g}$ is rotated by $\chi$ about a random azimuthal angle
$\varepsilon \in [0, 2\pi)$, and the impurity velocity becomes

$$
\mathbf{v}_a' = \mathbf{v}_a - \frac{m_b}{m_a + m_b}
  \left[\mathbf{g}\,(1 - \cos\chi) + \mathbf{h}\sin\chi\right],
$$

where $\mathbf{h}$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf{g}$, has the same magnitude, and
points in the direction set by $\varepsilon$. Only the impurity is updated. The background 
is fixed, so this is a test-particle model.

### Neutrals

Neutrals ($Z = 0$) are skipped, since they don't undergo Coulomb collisions.
Neutral–plasma interactions such as charge exchange or elastic scattering off
background neutrals are not included in Flan.

### Safeguards

A few limits keep the calculation well behaved in extreme corners of the
background:

| Quantity | Limit |
|---|---|
| $n_e$, $T_e$, $T_i$ | At least `min_ne`, `min_te` and `min_ti` |
| $\ln\Lambda$ | At least 1 |
| $g$ | At least $10^{-3}$ m/s, to avoid dividing by zero in $s$ |
| $\cos\chi$ | Clamped to $[-1, 1]$, to guard against rounding errors |

## When the model is valid

The Nanbu model rests on a few assumptions. Each one is satisfied in typical
edge and scrape-off layer plasmas, but each can break down.

**Valid when:**

- **The plasma is weakly coupled, $\ln\Lambda \gg 1$.** The model assumes
  that the deflection is dominated by many distant, small-angle collisions,
  the same assumption behind the Landau/Fokker–Planck collision operator. Typical
  edge plasmas have $\ln\Lambda \approx 10$–$15$, which is well within this regime.
- **The impurities are trace.** The background is treated as a fixed Maxwellian
  that the impurities don't modify, so momentum and energy given to the
  background are not fed back into it. This is fine as long as the impurity
  density is small compared to the plasma density.
- **The background is close to a Maxwellian.** Background velocities are sampled
  from a drifting, isotropic Maxwellian with a single temperature, so
  temperature anisotropy and non-Maxwellian tails are not captured.
- **The background changes little over one time step.** The plasma
  parameters are evaluated once at the start of the step and held fixed
  for the collision.

One thing the model does *not* require is a time step much smaller than the
collision time. The $\chi$ distribution is valid for any $s$, which is the
point of the model. In practice, Flan's time step is set by resolving the gyromotion in
the Boris push, which usually makes $s$ small anyway.

**Invalid or questionable when:**

- **$\ln\Lambda$ approaches a few or less.** This happens in very cold, dense
  plasmas, e.g., deeply detached divertor conditions. Large-angle collisions
  become important, and the small-angle picture is no longer accurate. If
  $\ln\Lambda$ falls below 1 it's clamped to 1, so results in these
  regions should be treated with caution.
- **The impurity concentration is high.** The impurities would noticeably
  change the background (and $n_i \ne n_e$), which the test-particle approach
  can't capture.
- **The background has more than one ion species, or the main ions aren't singly
  charged.** $q_b = e$ and $n_b = n_e$ are assumed for the ion collisions, so
  collisions with other ion species, e.g., impurity–impurity collisions, are not
  included.
- **Neutral collisions matter.** As noted above, only Coulomb collisions with
  charged background species are modeled.
