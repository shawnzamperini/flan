    Ionization and Recombination

    As an impurity moves through the plasma, its charge state changes through electron-impact ionization and recombination. Flan models both as random events whose probabilities come from OpenADAS rate coefficients. They are turned on with imp_iz_recomb = "on", and are applied once per time step, at the start of each pass through the main loop (see Impurity Transport).

    Rate coefficients

    The rate coefficients come from the OpenADAS database (open.adas.ac.uk), in ADF11 format:

    • SCD files: effective ionization rate coefficients, \(S_Z(n_e, T_e)\), for \(Z \to Z+1\).
    • ACD files: effective recombination rate coefficients, \(\alpha_{Z}(n_e, T_e)\), for \(Z \to Z-1\).

    These are effective (collisional-radiative) coefficients. They depend on both \(n_e\) and \(T_e\), and they include the effect of the excited-state populations. Recombination includes radiative, dielectronic and three-body recombination.

    Each file is read from

    [openadas_root]/scd[openadas_year]/scd[openadas_year]_[element].dat
[openadas_root]/acd[openadas_year]/acd[openadas_year]_[element].dat

    where openadas_root and openadas_year are input options (openadas_year defaults to 50) and the element is chosen with imp_atom_num. For example, tungsten with the default year reads scd50/scd50_w.dat and acd50/acd50_w.dat. The supported elements are H through Ne, Al, Si, Cl, Ar, Fe, Kr, Mo, Xe and W.

    The files store \(\log_{10}\) of the densities (cm\(^{-3}\)), temperatures (eV) and rate coefficients (cm\(^3\)/s). These are converted to m\(^{-3}\), eV and m\(^3\)/s when they are read in (OpenADAS::read_rate_coefficients in src/openadas.cpp).

    Interpolation

    For each particle, \(n_e\) and \(T_e\) are interpolated to the particle's position and time, the same way as in the Boris push. The rate coefficient for the particle's charge state is then bilinearly interpolated in \((T_e, n_e)\) from the table.

    If \(n_e\) or \(T_e\) fall outside the range of the OpenADAS data, they are clamped to the nearest edge of the table. The rates are never extrapolated.

    Charge-state update

    During one time step \(\Delta t\), a particle in charge state \(Z\) ionizes with probability

    \[ P_\text{iz} = S_Z(n_e, T_e)\, n_e\, \Delta t \]

    and recombines with probability

    \[ P_\text{rec} = \alpha_Z(n_e, T_e)\, n_e\, \Delta t. \]

    Two uniform random numbers \(U_1, U_2 \in [0, 1)\) are drawn, and the charge becomes

    \[ Z \to Z + [U_1 < P_\text{iz}] - [U_2 < P_\text{rec}], \]

    where \([\cdot]\) is 1 if the condition is true and 0 otherwise. If both events happen in the same step they cancel and the charge state is unchanged, so the charge changes by at most one per step.

    A fully stripped ion (\(Z\) equal to the atomic number) can't ionize, so \(P_\text{iz} = 0\). The velocity is not changed by an ionization or recombination event. The new charge simply takes effect in the next Boris push and collision step.

    The CPU version is OpenADAS::ioniz_recomb (src/openadas.cpp), and the GPU version is ioniz_recomb_kernel (src/cuda/openadas.cu).

    Neutrals

    Neutrals are handled correctly

    A neutral (\(Z = 0\)) can ionize to \(Z = 1\) using the \(Z = 0 \to 1\) SCD coefficient, but it can't recombine, so \(P_\text{rec} = 0\). Once it's ionized, it is pushed by the fields and starts to undergo collisions. See Impurity Transport and Collisions.

    Choosing the time step

    \(P = R\, n_e\, \Delta t\) is the first-order expansion of the exact probability \(1 - e^{-R\, n_e\, \Delta t}\), and it's only accurate when \(P \ll 1\). The update also allows at most one ionization or recombination per step. Both mean the time step must be short compared to the ionization and recombination times, \(1/(S_Z n_e)\) and \(1/(\alpha_Z n_e)\).

    If \(P > 1\), the event always happens and the true rate is underestimated. Flan counts how often this happens, and prints a warning at the end of the run:

    Warning: Ionization probability was > 1.0 for N particles. Consider using a smaller time step.

    (and similarly for recombination). The counter is incremented each time step that the probability is above 1, so \(N\) counts particle-steps rather than unique particles. If these warnings show up for more than a handful of particle-steps, reduce imp_time_step. This is most likely for low charge states in hot, dense plasma, where ionization is fastest.

    When the model is valid

    Valid when:

    • The time step resolves the atomic time scales, i.e., \(P \ll 1\) (see above).
    • The plasma is within the range of the OpenADAS tables. Outside the table, the edge values are used, which can be a poor approximation, e.g., at temperatures far below the lowest tabulated \(T_e\).
    • The electrons are close to Maxwellian. The ADAS coefficients are averaged over a Maxwellian electron distribution at \(T_e\).
    • The excited states are in quasi-steady state. The effective coefficients assume that excited-state populations relax much faster than the plasma conditions change along the impurity's path. This usually holds, though long-lived metastable states can violate it.

    Not included:

    • Charge-exchange recombination with background neutrals, e.g., \(W^{Z+} + D^0 \to W^{(Z-1)+} + D^+\), which can be important in regions with a high neutral density.
    • Ionization by ions or photons. Only electron-impact ionization is included.
    • More than one charge change per step. See Choosing the time step.
